\section{What Was Delivered}

A free, open-source, 107-page technical book taking a developer from the
mathematics of zero-knowledge proofs to a working, tested ZK smart contract on
Cardano, plus a runnable reference implementation. Nine chapters and a
sitography of the whole ZK-on-Cardano landscape. Built reproducibly in LaTeX via
Nix and GitHub Actions, in light and dark themes.

\vspace{-0.2em}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Book:} \texttt{eBook/light.pdf} and \texttt{eBook/dark.pdf}, 107
        pages each, published to the \texttt{rendered} branch on every push.
  \item \textbf{Reference implementation:} \texttt{zk-password/}, a
        password-locked UTxO unlocked by a Groth16 proof. Circom circuits,
        snarkjs trusted setup, point compression, Aiken verifier and validator,
        15 passing tests, on-chain cost benchmarks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Key Achievements: Measured, Not Asserted}

The project's most valuable output is a set of numbers nobody had published.
All are reproducible with \texttt{aiken check -m bench} in \texttt{zk-password/}.

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l r X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Finding} & \textbf{Value} & \textbf{Why it matters} \\
\midrule
Compiled Groth16 verifier       & 570 bytes                   & The on-chain part is tiny \\
Cost to verify a proof          & 2,005,373,690 steps         & $\approx$ 0.147 ADA, 20\% of the CPU budget \\
\dots\ and that cost is         & constant                    & Independent of circuit size \\
Max verifications per tx        & 4                           & Hard ceiling, CPU-bound \\
Cost floor per Plutus operation & $\approx 2\times10^5$ steps & Interpreter overhead, not operand width \\
On-chain Poseidon vs blake2b    & $\approx$ 1,500$\times$     & No optimisation escapes the floor \\
Depth-20 Poseidon Merkle insert & 120\% / 173\%               & Of CPU / memory budget: does not fit \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Headline conclusion.} Cardano can verify a zero-knowledge proof about
any computation, of any size, for about 0.147 ADA and 570 bytes of on-chain
code. It cannot yet maintain the kind of cryptographic state the most ambitious
ZK applications need, because it lacks a ZK-friendly hash builtin and the eUTxO
ledger deliberately has no shared mutable state. \textbf{A single Poseidon
builtin is the highest-leverage fix available.}
\end{keybox}

Chapter 8 applies this to a Tornado Cash design and reaches a clearly argued
negative result, identifying three walls: no ZK-friendly hash builtin; a
hash-function dilemma where the on-chain-cheap hash forces server-side proving
and therefore destroys the privacy; and eUTxO contention, where a pool's
anonymity set is capped by its throughput of one deposit per block. It also
documents where eUTxO beats Ethereum: minting a token named after the nullifier
hash makes double-spend prevention a ledger invariant, not a script check.

\section{Key Learnings}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The on-chain part is the small part.} A ZK application on
        Cardano is an off-chain application with a 570-byte on-chain referee.
        Off-chain logic needed a full chapter, not the half we budgeted.
  \item \textbf{Cardano's ZK cost profile is bimodal, not graded.} Cryptography
        with a builtin is nearly free; cryptography without one is effectively
        unavailable. No amount of careful Aiken bridges that gap.
  \item \textbf{Measuring changed our conclusions.} The original outline assumed
        ``too expensive, roughly.'' Measurement located the cost in interpreter
        overhead rather than bignum width, which rules out whole classes of
        proposed optimisation. Budget time for measurement, not only exposition.
  \item \textbf{Negative results are worth publishing.} Documenting why
        something cannot be built, with numbers, saves other teams weeks.
\end{itemize}

%---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Next Steps}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Add replay protection to the reference contract: a nonce in the datum
        and the circuit's public inputs. An afternoon's work, left as a reader
        exercise.
  \item Implement Poseidon in Aiken and publish real measurements, replacing our
        labelled lower-bound extrapolations with data.
  \item Argue for a Poseidon builtin using these numbers, published under
        AFL-3.0 precisely so they can be reused in a CIP or governance thread.
  \item Build with off-chain computation compression: constant verification cost
        means an arbitrarily large computation verifies for $\approx$ 0.147 ADA,
        with none of Chapter 8's walls. The best effort-to-value ratio available
        on Cardano today, and badly underexploited.
\end{itemize}

The repository stays open for issues and pull requests. Corrections are more
welcome than praise, and the Chapter 8 estimates in particular deserve to be
replaced by someone's real implementation.
